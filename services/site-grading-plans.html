Site plans and lot grading plans

Most building permits need a site plan, and many need a grading and drainage plan. We prepare both on our own survey, so the drawing the municipality reviews matches the ground.

Get a fixed-fee quote705 434 0411

When you need it

For your permit application

  • A new house, cottage, addition, garage or pool
  • A municipality that requires a lot grading and drainage plan
  • A septic system that has to be located and sized on a plan
  • A revision after the municipality's review comments
  • Earthworks that need volumes calculated

What you receive

Drawings ready for review

  • A site plan with setbacks, lot coverage and the zoning figures the municipality asks for
  • Existing and proposed elevations, swales and drainage directions
  • Revisions in response to municipal comments, as scoped in your proposal
  • PDF and CAD files for your builder and designer
  • Layout and certificates from the same firm when construction starts

Good to know

Start with a good survey

A grading plan drawn on poor base information tends to fail on site, when the driveway is steeper than drawn or water runs toward the neighbour. Ours start from a topographic survey we measured.

Each municipality has its own grading standards and review process. Tell us where the property is and what the building department has asked for.

If the design changes after the plan is submitted, we revise the plan. Changes to the scope are quoted as a fixed fee before we do them.

Common questions

Questions we hear most

Do I need a topographic survey first?

Yes. The grading plan is designed on top of it. We can quote both together.

Who decides whether I need a grading plan?

Your municipality. Many require one for new homes and large additions, and some for pools and septic replacements.

Will you deal with the municipality's comments?

Yes. Your proposal states what revisions are included.
