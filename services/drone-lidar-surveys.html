Drone, 3D scanning and bathymetric surveys

When a site is large, steep, complicated or under water, we use drones, laser scanners and sounding equipment to measure it quickly and completely.

When you need it

When a tripod is not enough

  • Mapping large or hard-to-reach sites from the air
  • A current orthophoto of a property or project
  • Stockpile and earthworks volumes
  • A 3D scan of a building, structure or accident scene
  • Depths and lake-bottom contours for docks, boathouses and shoreline work

What you receive

Measured data, not just pictures

  • Ground control surveyed by our crew, so the mapping is tied to real coordinates
  • Orthophotos, point clouds, surface models and contours
  • Volume reports with the method stated
  • Soundings reduced to a stated water level or datum
  • Files in the formats your engineer or designer uses

Good to know

The right tool for the site

Drones are fast over open ground and weak under tree cover. Scanners capture buildings in detail. Often the best result combines them with conventional survey. We choose the method after we understand the site.

Because the control is set by a survey crew, the results can be combined with a boundary or topographic survey of the same property.

Common questions

Questions we hear most

Is a drone survey accurate enough for design?

With surveyed ground control, yes for most open sites. Under trees or where fine detail matters we add ground measurements.

Can you show the property boundary on the orthophoto?

Yes, if we survey the boundary. Without that, a line drawn on an air photo is only approximate.

Do you survey under water?

Yes. We take soundings for docks, boathouses, dredging and shoreline applications.
