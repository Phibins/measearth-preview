Severances, easements and reference plans

When land is divided, added to a neighbour or made subject to an easement, the new parcel has to be described on a reference plan. We prepare the plan and deposit it at the Land Registry Office.

When you need it

When the shape of ownership changes

  • You are severing a lot and applying for consent
  • You and a neighbour are adjusting the line between you
  • You are granting or receiving an easement or right-of-way
  • You are buying the shore road allowance in front of your cottage
  • Your lawyer needs a legal description for a new parcel

What you receive

From application sketch to deposited plan

  • A severance sketch for your consent or minor variance application
  • Frontage, depth and area figures for the planner and the Committee of Adjustment
  • The field survey, with monuments set at the new corners
  • A reference plan (R-plan) showing each new part, signed by an Ontario Land Surveyor
  • Deposit of the plan at the Land Registry Office, and copies for you and your lawyer

Good to know

Plan the survey around the approval

A consent usually comes with conditions and a deadline. The reference plan has to be deposited before your lawyer can register the new deed, so tell us the lapse date as soon as you have it.

Municipalities often set minimum frontage and lot area. We check the figures against the by-law requirements you give us before the plan is finalized, because a plan that misses a minimum has to be redone.

For shore road allowances, the municipality decides whether and how the allowance can be closed. We survey to the water's edge they specify and prepare the plan they need.

Common questions

Questions we hear most

Do I need the survey before I apply?

Most applications need a sketch showing the proposed lots and their dimensions. The full reference plan normally follows once consent is granted with conditions.

What is the difference between a reference plan and a plan of subdivision?

A reference plan describes parts of existing land and is used for severances and easements. A plan of subdivision creates new lots and streets and goes through a longer approval.

Can you work with my planner and lawyer?

Yes. We deal with them directly so that the plan, the application and the deed all describe the same parcel.
